6 · Beginner's guide — read this first

Your daily 5-minute routine
  1. Check the market mood (box 1). GREEN “BULL” = you may buy. AMBER “CAUTION” = buy small, only strong signals. RED “BEAR” = do not buy anything new.
  2. Check your holdings (box 3). If it says SELL NOW, sell that stock — the stop-loss was hit. Do not “wait for it to come back”. That is how small losses become big ones.
  3. New buys (box 2). Buy the quantity shown, near the entry price. Immediately place a stop-loss order at the stop price in your broker app (Zerodha/Groww/Upstox all support “SL” or “GTT” orders).
  4. Taking profit. When price reaches Target 1, sell half. Move your stop-loss up to your buy price — now that trade can no longer lose money. Let the other half run; the dashboard will tell you when the trailing stop is hit.
  5. Record your trade in config.json → portfolio (symbol, buy price, quantity, date) so box 3 can track it.
How the algorithm decides (the score)

Each stock gets a score out of 100 from five independent pillars, recalculated every day:

  • Trend (30) — price vs 50/200-day averages, slope of the 50-day average, ADX trend strength.
  • Momentum (25) — RSI in the healthy 50–68 zone, MACD, and whether it beat the index over 3 and 6 months (relative strength).
  • Entry quality (15) — is price near its 20-day average (good) or stretched far above it (wait)? Bollinger %B and Stochastic.
  • Volume (10) — On-Balance-Volume trend and volume on up-days: are big buyers involved?
  • Risk (20) — low volatility, near its 52-week high, small daily range (ATR).

≥75 → STRONG BUY, ≥62 → BUY, 45–62 → HOLD, <45 → AVOID/SELL. Then the market filter overrides: in a BEAR market every BUY becomes WAIT. “Confidence” blends the score with how well these rules worked on that same stock historically.

How the stop-loss, targets and quantity are calculated
  • Stop-loss: just below the lowest price of the last 20 days, but never more than 2.5 × ATR (average daily move) below your entry, and never closer than 1 ATR (so normal noise doesn't kick you out).
  • Risk per share (R) = entry − stop. Target 1 = entry + 2R, Target 2 = entry + 3.5R. Each winner is worth 2–3.5× each loser, so you can be wrong half the time and still make money.
  • Quantity = (capital × risk %) ÷ R, capped at the max % of capital per stock. With ₹1,00,000 and 1% risk, a stop-loss hit costs you about ₹1,000 — not your whole account.
  • Trailing stop: after Target 1, the stop follows price up at (highest close − 3 × ATR), locking in profit.
Glossary
  • SMA 50 / 200 — average closing price of the last 50 / 200 days. Price above = uptrend.
  • RSI — 0–100 speed of price moves. Above 70 = overbought (expensive short-term), below 30 = oversold.
  • MACD — difference of two moving averages. Positive and rising = momentum up.
  • ADX — trend strength (not direction). Above 25 = strong trend.
  • ATR — the average size of one day's move in rupees. Used to size stops.
  • Drawdown — biggest fall from a peak. A −20% drawdown means the account fell 20% from its best value at some point.
  • Win rate / profit factor — % of trades that made money / total profits ÷ total losses (above 1.5 is good).
  • ETF — NIFTYBEES tracks the Nifty 50 index; GOLDBEES tracks gold. Simplest way for a beginner to invest.
⚠️ Honest truth about “no loss”

No algorithm in the world can guarantee zero losses — anyone who promises that is lying or running a scam. What this system does instead is the thing professionals actually do: keep every loss small and planned (stop-loss + 1% risk rule), let winners run (targets + trailing stop), and stay out of falling markets (regime filter). Expect roughly 35–55% of individual trades to be small losers; the profit comes from winners being much larger.

Start with a small amount you can afford to lose, or paper-trade for a month first. Past performance does not guarantee future results. This is an educational tool, not SEBI-registered investment advice.
