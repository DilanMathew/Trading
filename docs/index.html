8 · Beginner's guide

The idea in one minute

Stocks that have been rising faster than the rest tend to keep doing so for a while. This is called momentum, one of the most researched effects in finance. The system:

  1. Ranks ~160 large, liquid NSE stocks by relative strength (weighted return over 3, 6, 9 and 12 months).
  2. Only considers stocks above their 200-day average (in an uptrend) with enough daily trading.
  3. Holds the top 12, equal money in each. Every 2 weeks it checks again: a stock is kept while it stays in the top 18, otherwise it is swapped for a stronger one.
  4. Market-health switch: if the Nifty is below its 200-day average and fewer than half the stocks are rising, it sells everything and waits in cash. This is what limits big losses in crashes.
Your routine (10 minutes every 2 weeks)
  1. Look at box 2 on the rebalance day shown there (a Monday, once every two weeks).
  2. Sell everything listed under SELL. Then buy the BUY list with the quantity shown. Use "market" or "limit near current price" orders in Zerodha/Groww/Upstox. Delivery (CNC), not intraday.
  3. Do nothing with KEEP stocks. Between rebalance days, do nothing at all. Don't react to daily moves.
  4. Record buys in config.json → portfolio so box 4 can track them.
  5. If box 1 turns OFF on a rebalance day, sell everything and wait. You may park the cash in a liquid fund / LIQUIDBEES.
Why not BananaPatterns' VCP method?

VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern) looks for a stock in an uptrend whose pullbacks get smaller and smaller (e.g. −20%, −10%, −5%) while volume dries up, then buys when price breaks above the top of the last tight range on big volume. It's a well-known swing-trading method and the scanner (box 6) implements it.

We tested it honestly on 160 NSE stocks over 10 years. It gives few signals, many small losses from failed breakouts, and in 2022–2026 it barely made money. Ranking by relative strength and holding the leaders captured the same "strong stocks keep winning" idea more reliably, so it is the main engine. The VCP list is still useful: a stock that is both in the model and in a VCP is a high-quality leader.

Glossary
  • RS rank: relative strength percentile vs all stocks here (0–100).
  • Trend checks: 8-point "stage-2 uptrend" checklist (price above rising long-term averages, near 52-week high, etc.).
  • 200-day average: average price over ~10 months; above it = long-term uptrend.
  • Breadth: % of stocks above their 50-day average. A healthy market has many stocks rising, not just a few.
  • CAGR: average yearly growth rate. Max drawdown: worst fall from a peak.
  • Sharpe: return per unit of risk; above 1 is good.
⚠️ The honest truth about "no loss"

No method can guarantee zero losses. Anyone promising that is lying or running a scam. This system had losing years in testing and its worst fall was around −23%. What it does is keep you in the strongest stocks while the market is healthy and get you out when it isn't. Stick to it for years, not weeks, because momentum has bad stretches.

Start small (or paper-trade for 2 months). Never invest money you'll need soon. Educational tool, not SEBI-registered investment advice.
